# 17. Final Explain-It-to-Me Test — The Project Risk That Friday Catches

## 1. Explain it to a 15-year-old in three sentences

Someone promises an important document, but the deadline is coming and the document still is not there. Friday notices the gap, asks that person for the work, and alerts the project lead so they can help. It keeps checking and calls the problem solved only when there is evidence the work meets the needed standard.

## 2. Exactly what changes

| Without Friday | With Friday |
|---|---|
| The lead remembers the promise or discovers the missing document at the next status meeting. | Friday notices the potential risk while reviewing authorized work context. |
| The lead searches chat, notes, and the document location to reconstruct what happened. | Friday connects the commitment, history, people, dependency, and current artifact state. |
| The lead contacts the contributor and briefs whoever can unblock them. | Friday contacts the contributor and responsible lead when allowed, or requests confirmation at a safety/privacy boundary. |
| The lead has to return later and decide whether “done” really means done. | Friday follows the issue and checks current evidence against a derived acceptance standard. |

The feature owner confirms this full loop runs. Actual time and attention saved have **not yet been measured**.

## 3. One obvious example

**Illustrative, not a recorded customer result:** On Monday, Maya promises in a product-launch group to place the final compliance document in the agreed folder by Thursday at 17:00. Meeting minutes show that Alex leads the launch. When the document is still absent near the deadline and no later agreement changes the plan, Friday flags the risk, asks Maya for the document or blocker, and lets Alex know where help may be needed. If Maya replies “working on it,” the issue remains open; a verified final document that satisfies the launch requirement can close it.

## 4. One number in perspective

This example involves **three kinds of work source**—group discussion, meeting minutes, and a document location—and **two distinct human roles**—contributor and project lead. These are **illustrative scenario counts, not measured improvement**. Minutes saved, earlier contact time, correct-owner rate, and improved completion rate are **Not yet measured**. ## 5. Why the manual or generic AI method is harder

A person can do every step, but must remember the promise, check several places, contact both people, and return to verify the result. A generic LLM can summarize material that someone supplies, but a normal single prompt does not keep observing the work, deliver the messages, and own the follow-through. Structured project tools and newer AI teammates can cover parts of this job, sometimes extensively; a superiority claim over them needs a same-task comparison.

Include the permission decision and provider send result. A frontend concept screen is not a substitute for this proof.

## 7. The strongest 15–20 second story

| Time | Story beat | Evidence the frame must show |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 sec | An important launch document is due; nobody has flagged the missing deliverable. | Real, redacted commitment and due time. |
| 3–7 sec | Friday connects the promise, meeting owner evidence, and empty expected location. | Source-linked risk, not a generic alert. |
| 7–13 sec | Friday asks the contributor and alerts the lead. | Actual delivered messages or a clearly labeled approval step. |
| 13–17 sec | The issue receives a reply or artifact; Friday checks its acceptance standard. | Current evidence, not a claimed result. |
| 17–20 sec | The lead can focus on unblocking work while Friday keeps the follow-through visible. | Accurate resolved/open state. |

**Key message:** Friday does more than warn you about risk: it helps the right people act and checks whether the problem was actually resolved.
