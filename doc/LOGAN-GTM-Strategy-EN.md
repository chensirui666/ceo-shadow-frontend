# LOGAN Global Go-to-Market and Business Development Strategy

## From One User to Company-Wide Network Effects

## Mission and Vision

**Mission:** Free people from the operational work that consumes their time but not their talent.

**Vision:** Build a world where every person has a Second Shift — an AI that keeps work moving when they move on.

## 1. Category Thesis

LOGAN is the Second Shift: human do the "first shift" and people focus on high-value work that requires judgment, creativity, relationships, and decisions, while LOGAN finishes the "second shift" and handles the lower-value operational work they do not want to spend their time on—context recovery, coordination, follow-ups, recurring analysis, documentation, and execution.

Second Shift means AI takes over the lower-value operational work people do not want to handle themselves; it does not ask people to take on a second job.

The go-to-market model follows one principle: do not acquire users one by one. Acquire one high-leverage user, then let that person’s real work pull the rest of the company into the system.

> One user → one delegated job → recurring value → shared work → coworker participation → team adoption → organizational memory → enterprise expansion

The objective is not more invitations. Every valuable unit of work should create at least one opportunity for another person to experience LOGAN’s value.

## 2. Start With High-Leverage Users

LOGAN does not launch as “AI for everyone.” It starts with nodal operators: people whose work naturally touches many people, systems, and decisions.

| Priority | Initial ICP | Why this person is the best seed |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Founder / CEO | Highest context density across sales, hiring, delivery, and management |
| 2 | Chief of Staff / Operations Lead | Coordinates across teams and naturally pulls multiple workflows together |
| 3 | Head of Sales | Owns high-value customer context, commitments, and follow-ups |
| 4 | Project / Program Lead | Depends on many contributors, deadlines, and status inputs |
| 5 | Partner, investor, or professional-services lead | Repeated expert work with high information and relationship density |

The initial company profile is:

- 10–200 employees;
- distributed or cross-border teams;
- heavy use of Slack, Teams, Feishu / Lark, DingTalk, WhatsApp, and similar collaboration tools;
- high meeting and message volume;
- senior people who still manually coordinate important work; and
- repeated workflows that depend on personal context and expert judgment.

LOGAN enters global markets in a sequence designed for density rather than coverage:

| Wave | Focus |
|---|---|
| Wave 1 | US, UK, English-speaking European teams, and cross-border Asia teams |
| Wave 2 | DACH, France, Japan, Korea, and localized professional communities |
| Wave 3 | Enterprise and channel-led expansion through ecosystem partners |

The goal is not geographic breadth. It is dense pockets of high-fit users that can spread LOGAN inside their organizations.

## 3. Make Recurring Delegation the Activation Event

Registration is not activation. Connecting Slack is not activation. Chatting with LOGAN is not activation.

**The activation event:** a new user delegates at least one recurring job that is configured to run again within the first 72 hours.

Typical first workflows include:

- CEO Weekly Management Brief;
- Meeting → Actions → Owners → Deadlines;
- Customer Account Memory and Follow-Up;
- Project Risk and Commitment Tracking;
- Proposal / RFP Preparation;
- Investor and Portfolio Monitoring; and
- Cross-Group Daily or Weekly Summary.

The north-star activation metric is **Delegated Workflow Activation Rate**: the percentage of qualified new users who delegate at least one recurring workflow within 72 hours.

Onboarding should not begin with a blank chat. After approved context is connected, LOGAN should say: “Based on your work, here are three jobs I can take over.” The product must create the first “I no longer need to do this myself” moment as quickly as possible.

## 4. Build Viral Team Growth Into the Product

LOGAN grows through collaboration, not generic referral rewards. Every useful workflow should naturally require or benefit from another person viewing, contributing, approving, or acting.

| Growth mechanism | How it works | Growth effect |
|---|---|---|
| Action Owner Loop | Conversations become actions, and owners receive an actionable message before any complex setup | Each workflow creates qualified invitations |
| Shared Recipe | A high-value workflow becomes a reusable Recipe that colleagues can clone in their own account or project | Expert methods become distribution objects |
| Collaborative Approval | LOGAN requests approval for pricing, proposals, legal language, delivery timelines, or executive communications | Decision-makers experience value inside real work |
| Manager Digest Loop | LOGAN requests missing updates, consolidates inputs, and produces a team status report | One manager can naturally activate a team |
| Twin-to-Twin Handoff | Approved context, information requests, actions, and escalations move between coworkers’ LOGAN instances | Work dependencies create a reason to activate |
| Shared Team Memory | Commitments, project history, decisions, customer relationships, ownership, dependencies, and risks are accumulated | Incomplete context creates a natural pull toward expansion |
| Role-Based Expansion | Repeated dependencies reveal the next Sales, Delivery, or other role to add, with a preconfigured Recipe pack | The product identifies the next high-value user |
| Shareable Work Artifact | Weekly briefs, client plans, project-health reports, decision memos, and action trackers can be shared with controlled access | Work products become distribution outside the organization |
| Team Impact Dashboard | Workspaces see delegated workflows, completed deliverables, closed loops, completed follow-ups, and collaboration impact | Expansion is tied to measurable operating value |
| Internal Recipe Marketplace | Teams discover and reuse proven expert methods; selected Recipes can later be published | Experts become creators and distributors |

Team Memory should make context gaps visible. For example: “This workflow currently sees 42% of the context required. Add Sales and Delivery to complete the picture.” Expansion then becomes a product recommendation, not a sales pitch.

## 5. The Core Growth Loop

The target loop is:

1. A nodal user joins.
2. LOGAN understands approved context.
3. The user delegates a recurring job.
4. LOGAN produces visible, real work.
5. The work requires coworkers to view, approve, contribute, or act.
6. Coworkers experience value before sign-up.
7. Coworkers activate their own LOGAN instances.
8. Shared Memory and Recipes make every LOGAN more valuable.
9. More workflows move into the system.
10. The workspace becomes execution infrastructure for the organization.
11. Customer success becomes proof and distribution for the next organization.

The compounding advantage is simple: more users create more context; more context creates better execution; better execution creates more shared work; more shared work creates more users.

## 6. Phased GTM Plan

### Phase I: 0–100 Users

**Objective:** Find the small number of high-value workflows that users become dependent on.

**Motion:** Founder-led selling, warm introductions, existing customer and investor networks, handpicked design partners, concierge onboarding, and daily product feedback. This phase prioritizes high-quality learning over broad paid acquisition.

**Customer strategy:** Recruit 30 high-context nodal users as design partners, not 300 random testers. Every user must complete a real workflow; then use warm introductions, customer networks, and organic coworker expansion to grow the validated sample to 70+ qualified users.

**Success gate:**

- 70+ qualified users;
- 50%+ connect a real work source;
- 30%+ delegate a recurring job;
- 10+ weekly dependent users;
- 5+ users voluntarily share a Recipe or work artifact; and
- 3+ companies show organic coworker adoption.

The key proof is simple: a user brings a coworker into the workflow without being asked by Sales.

### Phase II: 100–1,000 Users

**Objective:** Make activation and internal expansion repeatable.

**Build:** Role-specific Recipe packs, shareable Recipes, action-owner invitations, collaborative approvals, Team Memory, one-click coworker activation, weekly impact reports, invite attribution, and viral analytics.

**Advance gate:** First delegation, recurring-workflow retention, and coworker activation occur repeatably across multiple high-fit accounts.

**Primary channels:**

1. LinkedIn for professional proof and role-specific use cases;
2. YouTube for deep workflow demonstrations;
3. founder-led X content for releases and build-in-public momentum;
4. specialist communities for RevOps, agency owners, Chiefs of Staff, PMO leaders, and founders; and
5. user sharing after real value has been demonstrated.

Every piece of content should show: **painful job → messy context → LOGAN executes → professional result → human approves → LOGAN remembers.**

### Phase III: 1,000–10,000 Users

**Objective:** Turn customers into the primary acquisition channel.

**Product growth:** Public or internal Recipe sharing, workflow-embedded team invites, shareable output pages, Twin-to-Twin collaboration, organizational Memory, role-based expansion recommendations, customer-created templates, and lightweight external guest participation.

**Creator engine:** Recruit practitioner-creators who use LOGAN on real workflows, rather than generic AI influencers. Prioritize founders and operators, RevOps, Chiefs of Staff, project managers, B2B sales leaders, agency operators, investors and analysts, and AI-automation educators. Optimize for activated workflows and retained users, not views.

**Community:** Use Discord or an equivalent community for onboarding, Recipe sharing, support, creator coordination, customer feedback, and product champions. Community is a retention and advocacy layer.

**Advance gate:** Active customers consistently generate newly activated users through work artifacts, Recipes, or collaborative invitations.

### Phase IV: 10,000–100,000 Users

**Objective:** Build a global distribution system around proven product-market fit.

**Product-led channels:** Team expansion, shared Recipes, public artifacts, invite loops, cross-company collaboration, and a Recipe marketplace.

**Search:** Create high-intent pages that launch directly into a relevant Recipe, including AI CEO Weekly Report, AI Sales Follow-Up, AI Project Status Report, AI RFP Assistant, AI Chief of Staff, AI Customer Memory, and AI Investment Due Diligence.

**Ecosystem and channels:** Prioritize Slack, Microsoft Teams, Feishu / Lark, DingTalk, WhatsApp, Discord, and emerging collaboration platforms. Recruit collaboration-platform consultants, AI implementation firms, RevOps agencies, MSPs, professional-services networks, and VC / accelerator ecosystems. Partners distribute a proven workflow, not a generic platform.

Every custom integration requires committed distribution: marketplace exposure, pilot customers, a joint campaign, or partner-led demand.

**Advance gate:** Product-led expansion, search, ecosystem, and channel sources form a stable, attributable acquisition mix.

### Phase V: 100,000–1M+ Users

**Objective:** Become the execution layer for how organizations work.

The enterprise motion is bottom-up: Individual Second Shift → Team Second Shift → Business Unit → Enterprise. The enterprise conversation begins after employees have demonstrated real work value.

Enterprise products include Organizational Memory, governance, permissions, audit trails, human approval policies, an enterprise Recipe library, private deployment, model choice, data boundaries, analytics, admin controls, and an API / developer platform.

At this stage, LOGAN is no longer only a productivity product. It becomes organizational execution infrastructure.

**Advance gate:** Organizational Memory, governance, and auditability support sustained adoption of critical workflows across teams.

## 7. Monetization and Account Expansion

| Tier | Core capability | Commercial purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Free | One LOGAN, one primary workflow, limited Memory and execution volume | First delegation and sharing |
| Pro | More Memory, Recipes, integrations, and execution volume | Individual dependence |
| Team | Shared Recipes, shared Memory, approvals, analytics, and collaboration | Workspace expansion |
| Enterprise | Governance, security, auditability, private deployment, and organizational intelligence | Institutional adoption |

The commercial journey is Individual Value → Collaborative Value → Organizational Value. The billing unit combines subscription tier and execution allowance; users upgrade when they need more Memory, Recipes, integrations, execution capacity, or team collaboration. Pricing is validated continuously through design-partner and high-fit-customer willingness to pay, usage depth, and unit execution cost. **Work DNA** is the user’s editable system of personal working standards and preferences. As adoption expands, LOGAN can also reach third-party SaaS, CRM, project-management tools, and internal systems through APIs and a developer platform that embed context understanding, Memory, task execution, and Work DNA into external workflows.

## 8. Global Growth Metrics

### Activation

- Time to First Delegated Job;
- Delegated Workflow Activation Rate;
- First-Week Completed Workflows; and
- Day-7 / Day-30 Workflow Retention.

### Virality

- Percentage of active users who expose a coworker to LOGAN;
- coworker invite conversion;
- shared Recipe clone rate;
- shared artifact-to-signup conversion;
- collaborative approval-to-activation conversion; and
- activated coworkers generated per active user.

### Team Expansion

- Users per workspace;
- 30-day team penetration;
- time from user #1 to user #3 and user #10;
- cross-functional workflow count; and
- workspace expansion rate.

### Commercial

- Paid-workspace conversion;
- expansion revenue;
- net revenue retention;
- CAC payback;
- revenue per workspace; and
- enterprise expansion velocity.

The long-term north-star metric is **Workspace Viral Coefficient**: activated coworkers generated per activated seed user. Above 1.0 in high-fit organizations, LOGAN creates compounding workspace expansion.

### Metric Definitions

- **Qualified user:** A user who matches the target role and company profile and completes basic workspace setup.
- **Real work source:** A user-approved Connector containing production message, calendar, file, or task data; demo data is excluded.
- **Recurring workflow:** A workflow configured to run again on a fixed cadence or in response to a defined event.
- **Weekly dependent user:** A user who views, uses, or relies on LOGAN to complete at least one work artifact during a continuous week.
- **Coworker exposure:** A non-user coworker who encounters LOGAN-generated work through a task, approval, shared artifact, or update request.
- **Workspace Viral Coefficient:** The number of activated coworkers attributable to a seed user’s workflows in the 30 days after that seed activates, tracked by high-fit account cohort.

## 9. Operating Principles

- Focus on high-context users and specific, high-value workflows.
- Validate workflow retention and collaborative value before widening distribution.
- Use work products, Recipes, approvals, and coworker collaboration as growth entry points.
- Tie ecosystem integrations to clear customer and channel distribution.
- Build credible content through practitioners and customer work outcomes.
- Expand customer value through the path: Individual → Team → Organizational Memory → Enterprise execution layer.

## 10. Investor / Panel Summary

LOGAN starts with high-context operators inside 10–200-person organizations and gets each new user to delegate a recurring job within 72 hours. Through action owners, collaborative approvals, shared Recipes, shareable work artifacts, and Team Memory, real work naturally reaches coworkers; coworkers experience value before sign-up and then activate their own LOGAN instances.

As more work enters the system, organizational context, expert methods, customer relationships, historical decisions, and feedback compound. This produces better execution and more retained organizational value, creating a growth path from individual value to team collaboration, Organizational Memory, and enterprise execution infrastructure.

> The fastest path to one million users is not acquiring one million individuals. It is making one user inside a company so valuable that the other 99 naturally need to join.
